🌞 The Sun

We know that life on our planet is possible thanks to the Sun: its energy allows for the development of plants, animals, and all kinds of living beings, and it affects the atmosphere and oceans. But we also know that certain solar radiation can harm our health: we must avoid sunburns and never look directly at the Sun.


The Sun is a star: a huge sphere of gas at high temperatures ("plasma"). It may seem calm and unchanging in the sky, however, the Sun is incredibly dynamic and is never the same from one day to the next.

Create and share

  • Do you want to know if there are sunspots on the Sun? Visit the website: www.spaceweatherlive.com and observe the shapes, sizes, colors, and quantity of sunspots live.
  • Navigate through the SpaceWeather website and observe the behavior of the Sun in previous days.
  • You can make a drawing of the Sun with the spots that you liked the most or that caught your attention.
  • Talk to your family and friends about your drawing and share it.

Did you know?

  • Compared to any of its planets, the Sun is immense: more than a million Earths could fit inside it.
  • We don't see the Sun as very large because it's very far away, about 150 million kilometers from Earth.
  • Our Sun won't live forever. Today it's approximately in the middle of its lifespan, and in about 5000 years it will expand to reach the orbits of Mercury and Venus.
  • The energy from the Sun's core takes a hundred thousand years to reach the surface and appear as light.
